# Rubric: is this plan ready to post and build from?

## Checks

| Check | Evidence | Pass condition | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| diagnosis-grounded | The plan's stated cause, read against the repro-evidence block in full — its steps, its artifacts, and especially its **control runs** and any numbered observation the reproduction isolates. Live: the plan's cause read against the student's posted repro comment on the issue. | The stated cause **explains the behavior the reproduction actually shows, and nothing in that reproduction rules it out**. **Fail** if any of these holds: (a) a **control run in the evidence contradicts the cause** — the evidence shows the named component behaving correctly under the same conditions, or shows the symptom surviving with that component removed, so the cause cannot be the one at work; (b) the cause **ignores a decisive observation** the evidence records, such as a timing matrix, an intermediate value, or a step showing the damage already done before the stage the plan blames; (c) the plan's cause is **inherited from the issue or the thread and the package's own evidence disagrees with it** — a confident third party does not outrank the reproduction. A plan that adopts the issue's cause and shows the evidence fitting it passes. Judge the fit between cause and evidence only, never the plan's confidence or length: a terse correct diagnosis passes and a long polished one that a control run has already excluded fails. | required |
| scope-bounded | The plan's in-scope and not-in-scope statements, its enumerated changes, and its named files or areas, read against what the issue reports and the reproduction isolates. | **Every change the plan proposes serves the reported defect.** A plan may be smaller than the issue and still pass: an explicit, reasoned deferral ("not in scope: option 1's larger rework, which the maintainers may prefer long term") is bounding, not evasion. **Fail** if the plan bundles **independently shippable work the issue did not ask for** alongside the fix — a dependency migration, a refactor or rewrite of the surrounding component, a new user-facing option or settings field, a UI change for an adjacent complaint, a CI or test-harness migration, or a redesign the fix is then buried inside. Phrases like "while touching this area" or "while I am in here" introducing further work are the signal to look for, but the test is the work itself, not the phrasing. **This check fails even when the plan's core fix is correct**: a right fix wrapped in four unrequested fronts is not one bounded change. A plan that fixes the defect at more than one site because the reproduction implicates more than one site is still bounded. | required |
| executable | The plan's named files, code sites, or areas, plus its approach and its order of work. | A stranger with repository access **could start the first step today without asking the author a question**. Both must hold: (1) the plan **names where the change goes precisely enough that a stranger knows which code to open** — specific files or functions, or a named component together with the specific path or call site within it ("the client attach path in `zellij-server`'s session connection handling, before pane input is wired"). A bare component or subsystem with no path through it ("the git modules", "the input stack") is not enough; and (2) the plan **commits to one operation**, so the central decision is made before the build starts. **Fail** if: (a) the first step is to **investigate, profile, or explore** in order to decide what the change will be; (b) the **layer or target is still open** ("gocui? tcell? not sure", "upstream or vendored, whichever is easier"); (c) **no files, code sites, or identified paths are named** at all; (d) the substantive decisions are **deferred to build time** ("recover() somewhere", "maybe also check the other linters"). An approach that names one chosen route and leaves a *secondary* detail open still passes: pinning exact function names after tracing with a diagnostic the author already has working is a secondary detail when the operation and its site are decided, whereas choosing *what to change* is the central decision and cannot be deferred. The test is always whether the first step is actionable, not how fine-grained the naming is. | required |
| test-plan-decisive | The plan's test plan, read against the repro evidence's steps and artifacts: what will be run, and what result would show the fix worked. | The test plan names **at least one observable outcome specific to this fix** that a reader could check and that would come out differently before and after the change. The strongest form, and the one to look for first, is **the reproduction re-run with the changed result stated** ("re-run the repro command, expect drawn output and exit 0", "at step 3 the color flips without leaving the view", "the three pattern spellings each print `C:\t\fixture\src\foo\a.spec.ts`"). A new regression test or fixture counts when the plan says **what it asserts**. **Fail** if: (a) the plan only names a **suite run** ("run the full test suite and make sure nothing regresses") with no outcome specific to the defect — a green suite is consistent with the bug still being there; (b) the stated outcome is an **impression rather than an observation** ("the prompt should feel fast", "nothing else should feel broken", "timings should look much better"); (c) there is **no test plan at all**. A suite run *alongside* a fix-specific observable passes; the suite is not the problem, the absence of a decisive observable is. | required |
| thread-and-conventions | The candidate plan comment, read against (1) the `## Thread highlights` section — who spoke, their association, and any direction they gave — and (2) the `## Repo facts` block's stated contribution policy and bug-report template asks. Live: the issue thread on GitHub and the repo's CONTRIBUTING and AI policy files. | **Both limbs must hold.** **(a) Thread direction.** If the thread carries **explicit direction from a maintainer** (an OWNER, MEMBER, or COLLABORATOR who names the culprit site, proposes or rejects an approach, posts a patch and asks for testing, or states a constraint on the fix), the comment **engages that direction** — follows it, or gives a reason for taking a different route. **Fail** if the comment proceeds as though the direction were not there: a maintainer isolating the culprit in a named source file and posting a test binary, answered by a comment that proposes documenting a workaround and never mentions either, fails this limb. A thread with no comments, or with no maintainer direction in it, passes limb (a) with nothing owed. Disagreeing with a maintainer is allowed; ignoring them is not. **(b) Stated conventions.** Treat the package as **AI-assisted work, because it is**; so a stated disclosure duty always applies. If the repo's policy **requires disclosing AI assistance** — in any form, anywhere, or in comments specifically — the comment must **say that AI assistance was used and in what way**. A policy that states **no AI rule**, that only asks for **responsibility, understanding, or testing**, that requires comments be in the contributor's **own words** (an own-words clause is not a disclosure clause), or that **explicitly exempts issue comments** from its disclosure ask, passes limb (b) automatically. | required |
| risks-stated | The plan's risk, unknown, or open-question statements, read against the change it proposes. | The plan names **at least one genuine risk or unknown of this change** and says what it would do about it — gate it, measure it, raise it in review, or leave it to a follow-up. A restated summary of the fix, or "no risks", does not count. | preferred |
| prior-art-engaged | The `## Thread highlights` section and the issue body for existing attempts — open or merged pull requests, a posted patch, a test build, another contributor already on the same route — read against what the plan comment says about them. | Where prior art exists, the comment **says how its work relates to it** — rebasing onto it, testing it, deferring to it, or explaining what it adds — rather than racing it silently. Where the thread shows no prior attempt, this check passes with nothing owed. | preferred |

## Verdict rule

**Accept** if every `required` check is `pass`. **Reject** if any `required` check is `fail`. An `unclear` grade on a required check counts as **fail**: a plan whose readiness I cannot establish from the package is a plan that is not ready to build from.

The two `preferred` checks (`risks-stated`, `prior-art-engaged`) **never change the verdict** in either direction. They separate a strong ready plan from a bare one, and a plan can be accepted without either.

Both limbs of `thread-and-conventions` must pass for that check to pass; a failure of either limb fails the check and therefore holds the package. When a check fails, the deciding limb or sub-condition is named in the output's evidence line.
